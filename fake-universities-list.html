6 Red Flags of a Fake University

1

Grand names

Words like United Nations, World Peace, Global or Spiritual in the name, with no law that created the university.

2

No DEB listing

The university or your programme is missing from the UGC-DEB entitled list for your session.

3

Personal payment

Fees are asked in a personal account or through an unknown link instead of the university's official account.

4

Guarantees

Promises of a guaranteed job, instant degree or a much shorter course duration.

5

Agent-only admission

Admission is possible only through a study-centre agent, never on the university's own official website.

6

Similar names

A name that is almost the same as a real university. Match the exact spelling on the official list.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many fake universities are there in India in 2026?

UGC's latest list, titled as of February 2026, names 32 institutions as fake or self-styled universities across 12 states and Union Territories, with Delhi having the most (12).

Are degrees from these universities valid?

No. Only universities established or recognised under the UGC Act, 1956 can award valid degrees. Degrees from listed institutions are not accepted for jobs or higher studies.

If a university is not on this list, is it approved?

Not necessarily. This list only names institutions UGC has called fake. For an online degree you must still confirm that the university and your programme are entitled by UGC-DEB for your admission session on deb.ugc.ac.in.

Where is the official UGC list?

UGC publishes the list on its website, ugc.gov.in. UGC may update it, so always check the official page for the latest version before you enrol.

What should I do if I already paid a fake university?

Stop further payments, keep all receipts and messages, and report it to the local police or the cyber crime portal. You can also write to UGC. Then talk to our counsellors about a genuine alternative.
