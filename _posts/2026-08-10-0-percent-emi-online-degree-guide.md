<p>One of the biggest reasons students hesitate before enrolling in an online degree is the upfront fee — total programme fees can range from ₹80,000 to over ₹3,00,000. That's where 0% EMI plans come in, and understanding how they actually work can save you from surprises later.</p><h2>What '0% EMI' Really Means</h2><p>In a genuine 0% (no-cost) EMI plan, the total amount you pay across all instalments equals the original programme fee — no interest is added. The university or its financing partner absorbs the interest cost, often as part of a tie-up with a bank or NBFC.</p><h2>How the Process Works</h2><ul><li><strong>Step 1 — Eligibility check:</strong> Most plans require a valid PAN card, basic KYC documents, and sometimes a credit check or co-applicant for larger amounts.</li><li><strong>Step 2 — Tenure selection:</strong> Typical tenures range from 6 to 24 months. Longer tenures mean smaller monthly instalments.</li><li><strong>Step 3 — Processing fee:</strong> Some financing partners charge a one-time processing fee (usually 1-2% of the loan amount) even on a 'no-cost' plan — always ask for this upfront.</li><li><strong>Step 4 — Auto-debit setup:</strong> EMIs are usually collected via auto-debit (NACH mandate) from your bank account each month.</li></ul><h2>Questions to Ask Before You Sign Up</h2><ol><li>Is the processing fee included in the total cost comparison, or is it extra?</li><li>What happens if you miss an EMI — is there a penalty or does the 0% offer get revoked?</li><li>Is the EMI plan linked to your admission, or can you cancel/pause it if you discontinue the course?</li></ol><p>Use our <strong>0% EMI Planner</strong> on this website to estimate your monthly instalment instantly based on the total fee and tenure you choose.</p><h2>Bottom Line</h2><p>0% EMI can genuinely make a good online degree affordable — just read the fine print on processing fees and penalty clauses so there are no surprises after admission.</p>
