<p>Every admission season, fake universities and misleading online programmes try to catch students with low fees, fast degrees and big promises. A degree from an unrecognised institution can be useless for jobs and higher studies, and the money is often hard to recover. This guide gives you <strong>7 simple checks to verify whether a university is genuine</strong>, plus the red flags that should make you stop.</p>

<h2>Check 1: Is the University on UGC's Recognised List?</h2>
<p>A university in India must be established by a central, state or provincial Act, or be declared a deemed-to-be university. Search the name on the UGC website (ugc.gov.in), under the list of universities, and filter by state and type. If you cannot find it, treat that as a serious warning. Names that sound similar to a famous university are a common trick, so match the exact name and location.</p>

<h2>Check 2: Is It on UGC's Fake List?</h2>
<p>UGC publishes a list of institutions it has declared fake. Reports in 2026 put the latest list at 32 institutions across 12 states and Union Territories, with Delhi having the largest number. Earlier lists named 21 to 22 institutions, and counts differ between reports, so always check the live page on the UGC site. Our post on the <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/09/26/ugc-fake-universities-list-2026/' | relative_url }}">UGC fake universities list</a> explains the list in detail.</p>

<h2>Check 3: For Online or Distance Learning, Is the Programme UGC-DEB Entitled?</h2>
<p>Being a real university is not enough. For online and ODL programmes, the university must be recognised or entitled by the UGC Distance Education Bureau (UGC-DEB) for that programme and session. Verify on deb.ugc.ac.in, and read our explainer on <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/07/28/ugc-deb-approval-explained/' | relative_url }}">UGC-DEB approval</a>. UGC has also issued notices about fake online programmes with misleading abbreviations, so read the full programme name.</p>

<h2>Check 4: Does the Programme Need Another Council's Approval?</h2>
<p>Some fields need approval from other bodies. For example, AICTE's policy does not permit ODL or online courses at diploma, degree or postgraduate level in engineering and technology, and some other fields. UGC's regulations also prohibit certain programmes, such as medical and legal courses, from being offered in online or distance mode. If an online "B.Tech" or "MBBS" is offered, that is a red flag.</p>

<h2>Check 5: Is the DEB-ID and ABC-ID Process Part of Admission?</h2>
<p>UGC requires learners in ODL and online programmes to create a DEB-ID, generated with an ABC-ID. A university that never mentions this should be questioned. Read our guide on the <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/09/30/deb-id-mandatory-online-degree-admission-2026/' | relative_url }}">mandatory DEB-ID</a> and on <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/09/24/abc-id-academic-bank-of-credits-explained-2026/' | relative_url }}">ABC-ID</a>.</p>

<h2>Check 6: Is the Website, Address and Contact Genuine?</h2>
<ul>
<li>Open the university's official website from the UGC or DEB list, not from an ad or a forwarded link.</li>
<li>Look for a real campus address, phone and email, plus a mandatory disclosure or UGC approval document.</li>
<li>Be careful with lookalike domains and social pages that copy the real logo.</li>
<li>UGC has also warned against fake advertisements using the name of foreign universities that are not authorised to offer degrees in India.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Check 7: Is Payment Going Only to the University?</h2>
<p>Pay only through the official payment link on the university's own website, and keep every receipt. UGC regulations do not allow universities to run ODL or online programmes through franchise arrangements, so be wary of agents or "study centres" claiming to admit students on a university's behalf. Read the refund policy before paying.</p>

<h2>Red Flags That Should Make You Stop</h2>
<ul>
<li>A degree in a few months, or "guaranteed admission with any marks".</li>
<li>Fees that are far below comparable programmes, or pressure to pay today.</li>
<li>An agent who cannot show UGC or UGC-DEB recognition for your exact programme.</li>
<li>Requests to pay into a personal account.</li>
<li>No physical address, or a name that closely copies a well-known university.</li>
<li>An online programme in a field that cannot legally be offered online.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What to Do If You Have Already Paid a Fake Institution</h2>
<ol>
<li>Stop further payments and save all proofs: receipts, chats, ads, website screenshots.</li>
<li>If money was lost to fraud, report it on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the helpline 1930 as soon as possible.</li>
<li>Inform UGC and, for online programmes, UGC-DEB, so they can act.</li>
<li>Ask your bank about a dispute if you paid by card or UPI.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>How can I check if a university is fake?</h3>
<p>Search the exact name on the UGC recognised list, check the UGC fake universities page, and for online programmes verify UGC-DEB entitlement for the programme and session.</p>

<h3>How many fake universities are there in India?</h3>
<p>Reports in 2026 mention 32 institutions on UGC's latest list, up from 21 to 22 in earlier lists. The number changes, so check the live UGC page.</p>

<h3>Is a degree from a fake university valid for jobs?</h3>
<p>No. Degrees from institutions not recognised under the UGC Act are not valid for employment or further studies.</p>

<h3>Can a private university be genuine?</h3>
<p>Yes. Many private universities are established by state Acts and are recognised. Verify each one.</p>

<h3>Is an online degree from a real university always valid?</h3>
<p>Only if the university and the specific programme are UGC-DEB entitled for your session. Use our <a href="{{ '/ugc-verifier.html' | relative_url }}">checker</a> to confirm.</p>

<h2>Final Word</h2>
<p>Take a few minutes to verify before you pay. Check the UGC lists, confirm UGC-DEB for online programmes, and be cautious about agents and urgent offers. A genuine university will give you the details in writing.</p>
