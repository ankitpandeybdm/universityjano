<p>The August 2026 admission window has just closed, and many students are quietly asking the same question: "I paid the fee, but I want to cancel. Can I get my money back?" The answer depends on one thing most students do not know about, which is the <strong>UGC fee refund policy</strong>. It sets how much of your fee a university must return, based on when you withdraw compared with the university's last date of admission. If you are planning to join the <strong>January 2027 batch</strong>, understanding this rule before you pay can protect your money.</p>

<h2>Quick Answer: How Much Refund Can You Get?</h2>
<p>Under UGC's refund policy, you can get a full refund if you withdraw 15 days or more before the formally notified last date of admission, and the refund drops in steps after that. It reaches zero if you withdraw more than 30 days after the last date. A small processing fee can be deducted from the full-refund slab.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Best time to cancel:</strong> at least 15 days before the university's last date of admission.</li>
<li><strong>What decides the slab:</strong> the date you submit your withdrawal request, compared with the university's notified last date.</li>
<li><strong>Always do it in writing,</strong> and keep your receipts.</li>
</ul>

Since the details can change by session, ask your university to confirm in writing which refund policy it follows.</p>

<h2>What Is the "Last Date of Admission"?</h2>
<p>It is the date the university has formally notified as the last date for admission in that session. It is not the date you paid and not the date you joined. For the August 2026 session, UGC extended the last date for online and ODL admissions to 13 October 2026, as we covered in our post on the <a href="/blog/2026/10/04/ugc-extends-online-odl-admission-last-date-13-october-2026/">UGC extension</a>. Some universities notify their own date, so always check the one on your university's notice.</p>

<h2>How to Claim Your Refund: Step by Step</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Check the dates.</strong> Find the university's notified last date of admission and work out which slab you are in.</li>
<li><strong>Apply in writing.</strong> Send a withdrawal and refund request by email and through the university's portal or office. Keep a copy and the sent proof.</li>
<li><strong>Quote the rule.</strong> Mention that you are requesting a refund as per the UGC fee refund policy, with your enrolment number and payment receipts.</li>
<li><strong>Give bank details</strong> for the refund, and ask for the expected timeline in writing. Institutions are generally expected to process refunds within about 15 days of the written request, though the transfer itself can take longer.</li>
<li><strong>Follow up</strong> after a week with a reminder, quoting your first request.</li>
<li><strong>Escalate if needed.</strong> Use the university's grievance cell, then UGC's student grievance channel, and you can also contact the National Consumer Helpline on 1915.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Processing Fee: What Can the University Deduct?</h2>
<p>A university may deduct a processing fee from the full refund. UGC's recent notices have capped it at ₹1,000 for withdrawals up to the notified cut-off dates, while older guidelines refer to a percentage-based cap. Ask the university for the exact deduction in writing before you apply, and challenge any charge that looks higher than UGC allows.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Does the UGC refund policy apply to online degrees?</h3>
<p>Reports indicate that UGC's refund guidelines apply across regular, distance and online programmes at UGC-recognised institutions. Confirm with your university, in writing, which policy it follows.</p>

<h3>Can I get a 100% refund?</h3>
<p>Yes, if you withdraw at least 15 days before the university's formally notified last date of admission. A small processing fee can be deducted.</p>

<h3>What if I cancel after the last date?</h3>
<p>You can still get 80% if you withdraw within 15 days after the last date, and 50% between 16 and 30 days after it. After that, no refund is due under the UGC slabs.</p>

<h3>How long does a refund take?</h3>
<p>Institutions are generally expected to process it within about 15 days of your written request. Bank transfers may take longer, so follow up if you hear nothing.</p>

<h3>Is the refund based on the date I paid?</h3>
<p>No. It is based on the date you submit your withdrawal request, compared with the university's notified last date of admission.</p>

<h3>Where can I complain if my refund is refused?</h3>
<p>Start with the university's grievance cell, then UGC's student grievance channel. You can also contact the National Consumer Helpline on 1915.</p>
