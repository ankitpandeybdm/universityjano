<p>Can you do an online MBA while you are still finishing a BCom? Can you study an online BBA and an online BCA together? Until a few years ago, the honest answer was "not officially". Today, UGC's dual degree guidelines allow students to pursue <strong>two academic programmes at the same time</strong>, but only in specific combinations and under conditions that many students miss. This guide explains the rule in plain language, what changed in 2025, and how to avoid the mistakes that make a second degree a risk instead of an advantage.</p>

<h2>Quick Answer: Does UGC Allow Two Degrees at the Same Time?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Yes.</strong> UGC's guidelines on pursuing two academic programmes simultaneously permit it.</li>
<li><strong>Three combinations are allowed:</strong> two full-time regular programmes (with no timetable clash), one regular programme plus one online or ODL programme, or up to two online or ODL programmes.</li>
<li><strong>Online and ODL programmes must come from institutions recognised</strong> by UGC, a statutory council or the Government of India.</li>
<li><strong>PhD is excluded.</strong> The guidelines apply to programmes other than PhD.</li>
<li><strong>Universities must set up their own mechanism,</strong> so always confirm that both of your universities accept the arrangement.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The Rule in Brief: What UGC Actually Says</h2>
<p>UGC first issued guidelines for pursuing two academic programmes simultaneously in April 2023, following the NEP 2020 push for flexible, multidisciplinary learning. The guidelines were revised after the Commission's 589th meeting on 3 April 2025. The core permissions are:</p>

<p>The guidelines cover degree and diploma programmes. Together with the compulsory link to recognised institutions, this means a UGC-DEB approved online degree can legitimately sit alongside a second programme.</p>

<h3>What changed in the 2025 revision</h3>
<p>The most talked-about change was about the past. As reported, the revised guidelines recognise programmes that a student pursued simultaneously <em>before</em> the guidelines were issued, provided the student followed the UGC rules and the university statutes that applied at that time. The earlier 2023 version had said no retrospective benefit could be claimed. If you already hold two degrees earned in overlapping years, check your own case with your university rather than assuming either way.</p>

<h2>Conditions You Must Meet</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Both institutions must be recognised.</strong> For any online or ODL programme, the university must hold current UGC-DEB recognition or entitlement for that exact programme and session. See our guide to <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/10/19/ugc-entitled-vs-ugc-recognised-online-degree-2026/' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">UGC entitled vs UGC recognised</a>.</li>
<li><strong>No timetable clash for two regular programmes.</strong> Online programmes are more flexible, but you still need to manage live sessions and exams.</li>
<li><strong>Professional programmes follow their councils.</strong> Programmes under statutory or professional councils must also follow those councils' regulations.</li>
<li><strong>Your universities must allow it.</strong> UGC asked universities to devise mechanisms through their own statutory bodies, so one university may have a clear process while another does not.</li>
<li><strong>Each admission must meet its own eligibility.</strong> You need to qualify for each programme separately, with its own fees, documents and exams.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Is It a Good Idea? Honest Pros and Cons</h2>
<h3>When it makes sense</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>You are a regular student who wants a skill upgrade.</strong> A regular degree plus an online programme in a different field can broaden your profile.</li>
<li><strong>You want to switch fields without waiting.</strong> Starting an online programme in a new field while you finish your current degree saves time.</li>
<li><strong>The two programmes complement each other,</strong> such as commerce plus analytics, or arts plus management.</li>
</ul>
<h3>When to think twice</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Workload doubles.</strong> A single online programme usually needs roughly 6 to 10 hours a week, so two can need double that on top of a job or regular classes. <li><strong>Exam clashes are possible.</strong> Two universities can schedule proctored online exams on the same days. Read how <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/10/19/how-online-exams-work-online-degree-india-proctored/' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">online exams work</a> and ask for each exam calendar before you pay.</li>

<li><strong>Two weak degrees are worse than one strong one.</strong> If your attention is split and your marks suffer, the second degree can cost you more than it gives.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Does a Second Degree Count for Jobs and Exams?</h2>
<p>The dual degree rule says you may study two programmes together. It does not change how a particular recruiter, exam or university treats the degrees. Whether a degree counts for a government job, UGC NET or admission to a higher programme depends on that body's own notification. Read the eligibility clause for each, and see our guides on <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/08/24/online-degree-government-job-eligibility-2026/' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">government job eligibility</a> and <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/08/26/ugc-net-eligibility-online-degree-2026/' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">UGC NET eligibility</a>.</p>

<h2>DEB ID and ABC ID When You Study Two Programmes</h2>
<p>Online and ODL learners need a <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/09/30/deb-id-mandatory-online-degree-admission-2026/' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">DEB ID</a> and an <a href="{{ '/blog/2026/09/24/abc-id-academic-bank-of-credits-explained-2026/' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">ABC ID</a>. These IDs belong to you, not to a programme, so you create them once. Ask each university how it records a second concurrent enrolment, and keep copies of both admission records and fee receipts.</p>

<h2>Dual Degree vs Double Major vs Integrated Degree</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Dual degree:</strong> two separate programmes, two admissions, two syllabi, two degrees. This is what the UGC guidelines cover.</li>
<li><strong>Double major:</strong> one degree with two subject tracks, usually inside a single university.</li>
<li><strong>Integrated degree:</strong> a combined programme, such as a bachelor's and master's in one continuous course.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not mix these up when a university advertises a "dual" option. Ask whether you will get <em>two certificates</em> or one.</p>

<h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Assuming "UGC allows it" means "my university allows it."</strong> Get written confirmation from both.</li>
<li><strong>Enrolling in an unapproved online programme</strong> because the other degree is genuine. Both must be recognised.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring exam dates.</strong> A clash can cost you a semester.</li>
<li><strong>Taking on two programmes in the same field</strong> with heavy overlap, which adds workload but little new value.</li>
<li><strong>Relying on advertisements.</strong> Verify every claim on the official UGC and UGC-DEB websites.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Can I do two degrees at the same time in India?</h3>
<p>Yes. UGC allows two regular programmes with no timetable clash, one regular plus one online or ODL, or up to two online or ODL programmes, as long as the institutions are recognised.</p>

<h3>Can I do two online degrees together?</h3>
<p>Yes, up to two online or ODL programmes at a time, provided each is from a recognised university and approved for the session you join.</p>

<h3>Can I do a regular degree and an online degree together?</h3>
<p>Yes. One full-time regular programme plus one online or ODL programme is an allowed combination. Check the regular college's attendance rules.</p>

<h3>Do both degrees have to be from the same university?</h3>
<p>The guidelines do not require it. For online and ODL programmes you are not tied to one city, but each university must be recognised and willing to admit you. Confirm this with each one.</p>

<h3>Does the dual degree rule apply to PhD?</h3>
<p>No. The guidelines exclude PhD programmes.</p>

<h3>Can I do two degrees from two different states?</h3>
<p>Generally yes for online and ODL programmes, as long as both institutions are recognised and the schedules work. For two regular programmes, timetable and location make it hard in practice.</p>

<h3>Will my second degree be valid for government jobs?</h3>
<p>Validity depends on UGC-DEB approval for the online programme and on each recruiter's own eligibility notification. Check the notification before you rely on it.</p>

<h3>Is the dual degree rule new in 2026?</h3>
<p>No. UGC issued the guidelines in April 2023 and revised them in April 2025. We did not find a newer notification at the time of writing, so check the official UGC website for any update.</p>

<h2>The Bottom Line</h2>
<p>UGC allows two degrees at once, and for working students the online route is the easiest way to use it. But the permission is only the starting point. The real questions are whether both programmes are properly approved, whether both universities will actually admit you, and whether you can handle the workload without hurting your results. Verify first, ask in writing, then enrol.</p>
