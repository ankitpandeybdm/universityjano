<p>Almost every online university advertisement now carries a NAAC badge: "NAAC A+", "NAAC A++", sometimes with a score like 3.6 out of 4. Students are told it proves quality, but few know why <strong>3.26</strong> is the magic number in online education. It comes straight from the UGC's online and ODL regulations, and it decides which universities can start online degrees without prior UGC approval. This guide explains the rule in plain language for the <strong>January 2027 batch</strong>: what the 3.26 threshold means, how online and ODL thresholds differ, why A+ and A++ both clear it, what NAAC does <em>not</em> tell you, and how to verify a university's claim before you pay.</p>

<h2>Quick Answer</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The rule:</strong> under Regulation 3(B)(a) of the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, higher education institutions with a <strong>NAAC score of 3.26 and above</strong>, or a <strong>NIRF rank in the top 100 in the University category at least twice in the three preceding cycles</strong>, are permitted to start full-fledged online programmes without prior UGC approval.</li>
<li><strong>ODL is easier:</strong> for dual-mode institutions, the ODL threshold is a NAAC score of 3.01 and above, or a top-100 NIRF rank at least once in the two preceding cycles. For open universities, any NAAC score is accepted.</li>
<li><strong>What 3.26 means in grades:</strong> published explainers say an A+ grade corresponds to a score of 3.26 or more, which is why A+ and A++ both clear the online threshold.</li>
<li><strong>Not enough on its own:</strong> a NAAC grade tells you the institution may be eligible. You still need the <em>programme and session</em> to be approved on the UGC-DEB portal, and the discipline to be allowed online.</li>
<li><strong>Verify:</strong> check the grade, score and validity on the NAAC website, not on an advertisement.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The Rule in Plain Language</h2>
<p>UGC wants online degrees to come from institutions with a track record of quality. So it sets a gate, based on two independent measures:</p>

<p>Institutions that clear the online condition are described as <strong>entitled</strong> to offer online programmes, meaning they may start online programmes without prior UGC approval, provided they satisfy all the other conditions in the regulations. Published summaries add that such institutions are also permitted to enrol international learners, and that science programmes needing hands-on experience can be offered only by institutions with a NAAC score of 3.26 and above, by open universities, or by institutions with the required NIRF rank. <h2>A+, A++ and the 3.26 Score</h2>
<p>NAAC grades institutions on a four-point scale. Published explainers state that an <strong>A+ grade means a score of 3.26 or more</strong>, the level UGC requires before a university can run full online degrees. That has two practical consequences:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A+ and A++ both clear the online threshold.</strong> The difference between them matters for quality perception, but not for the basic UGC condition. This is why our reviews of <a href="/blog/2026/11/17/nmims-online-mba-2027-fees-eligibility-honest-review/" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">NMIMS</a>, <a href="/blog/2026/11/17/chandigarh-university-online-mba-2027-fees-dual-specialisation-review/" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">Chandigarh University</a> and <a href="/blog/2026/11/17/amity-online-mba-2027-fees-specialisations-honest-review/" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">Amity</a>, where pages quote A+ in some places and A++ in others, matter more for accuracy than for eligibility.</li>
<li><strong>A lower grade does not automatically rule a university out.</strong> A NIRF top-100 rank is an alternative route, and open universities are exempt from the NAAC condition.</li>
</ul>
<p>Whatever grade you see, the number that counts for UGC is the score on the valid certificate. A badge on a website is not evidence.</p>

<p>One education guide makes the same point: NAAC is an accreditation body set up by UGC to grade institutional quality, a university can be UGC-recognised without a high NAAC score, and the DEB decides each academic year which universities may offer online or ODL programmes, with approval renewed annually rather than granted permanently.</p>

<h2>Why the Score Must Be Valid and Recent</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The condition applies "at the time of application".</strong> A grade that has expired or lapsed does not count.</li>
<li><strong>Look at the certificate's validity dates,</strong> not only the grade letter.</li>
<li><strong>Check the cycle.</strong> Some pages quote an older cycle's grade. Confirm that the certificate on the NAAC site matches what is advertised.</li>
<li><strong>Be alert to format changes.</strong> If an institution shows a result in a different format from the familiar A++ or A+ scale, ask it to show in writing how the result meets UGC's condition for online programmes.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common Myths</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>"NAAC A++ means a better degree than A+ for jobs."</strong> Both clear the UGC condition. Recruiters and exams care about validity and your skills.</li>
<li><strong>"No NAAC means fake."</strong> Not necessarily. Open universities are exempt, and the NIRF route exists. But you still must verify.</li>
<li><strong>"NAAC A+ means every programme is approved."</strong> Approval is per programme and session.</li>
<li><strong>"A big grade means big placements."</strong> NAAC does not measure placement outcomes.</li>
<li><strong>"The advertised score is the current one."</strong> Check the certificate and dates.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What is the NAAC 3.26 rule for online degrees?</h3>
<p>Under Regulation 3(B)(a) of the UGC ODL and Online Programmes Regulations, 2020, institutions with a NAAC score of 3.26 and above, or a NIRF top-100 rank in the University category at least twice in three preceding cycles, may start online programmes without prior UGC approval.</p>

<h3>Is NAAC A+ enough to offer online degrees?</h3>
<p>Published explainers say A+ corresponds to a score of 3.26 or more, which meets the NAAC condition. You still need the programme and session to be approved, and the discipline to be allowed online.</p>

<h3>What is the NAAC requirement for ODL programmes?</h3>
<p>For dual-mode institutions, a score of 3.01 and above, or a top-100 NIRF rank at least once in two preceding cycles. Open universities may have any NAAC score.</p>

<h3>Can a university without NAAC offer online degrees?</h3>
<p>Open universities are exempt from the NAAC condition, and the NIRF top-100 route is an alternative. Others need UGC approval. Always verify on the DEB portal.</p>

<h3>Is NAAC A++ better than A+ for an online degree?</h3>
<p>Both clear the UGC threshold. A++ signals a higher institutional score, but validity and your skills matter more for jobs.</p>

<h3>Where do I check a university's NAAC grade?</h3>
<p>On the NAAC website, where you can see the score, grade and validity period. Do not rely on an advertisement.</p>

<h3>Does NAAC approve my specific programme?</h3>
<p>No. NAAC grades the institution. Programme approval for online mode comes from UGC-DEB, and AICTE applies to programmes such as MBA and MCA.</p>

<h3>What if a university's NAAC grade has expired?</h3>
<p>The condition applies at the time of application, so ask the university for the current certificate and check it on the NAAC site before paying.</p>
