<p>If you've enrolled in an online degree recently, you were probably asked for something called an ABC ID somewhere in the admission process — and if you're like most students, you filled it in without really knowing what it does. That's a missed opportunity. Your ABC ID is quietly one of the more useful pieces of infrastructure NEP 2020 introduced, and understanding it properly can save you real trouble if you ever pause your degree, switch universities, or want to combine credits from more than one institution.</p>

<h2>What ABC ID Actually Is</h2>
<p>ABC stands for <strong>Academic Bank of Credits</strong> — a digital system run under the University Grants Commission that stores the academic credits you earn during your degree, the way a bank stores money. Every student gets a unique ABC ID (a 12-digit number), and every UGC-recognized institution you study at can deposit credits into that account as you complete courses and semesters. It's linked to your APAAR ID (Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry) and accessed through DigiLocker, so the whole thing sits inside infrastructure you may have already used for other government services.</p>
<p>Here's the part worth remembering: <strong>you can't add credits to your own account.</strong> Only recognized institutions that are registered on the ABC platform can deposit credits after you've actually completed the coursework. Your ABC ID is a record, not something you self-report — which is exactly what makes it useful as a verifiable academic history.</p>

<h2>Why This Actually Matters for Online Degree Students Specifically</h2>
<p>Working professionals doing an online MBA or MCA are exactly the group ABC was built for — the students most likely to face a genuine reason to pause, switch, or combine study across institutions. Three practical scenarios where your ABC ID does real work:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Multiple Entry-Exit:</strong> if you need to pause your degree mid-way — a job change, a health issue, a relocation — your earned credits stay banked under your ABC ID. When you resume, whether at the same university or (subject to that university's own transfer policy) a different one, those credits don't vanish.</li>
<li><strong>Credit Transfer Between Universities:</strong> if your circumstances change and a different UGC-DEB approved university genuinely fits your situation better partway through, ABC is the mechanism that makes carrying completed credits across institutions possible — though both the sending and receiving institution need to actually support the transfer workflow, so this isn't automatic just because ABC exists.</li>
<li><strong>Combining Credits From Different Programs:</strong> ABC is designed to eventually support earning credits from more than one recognized institution or format and combining them toward a single qualification — genuinely useful if, say, you complete a short certification at one institution and want it to count toward requirements elsewhere, subject to each institution's specific credit-recognition rules.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to Get Your ABC ID</h2>
<p>The process is genuinely simple and free:</p>
<ol>
<li>Visit the official ABC portal at abc.gov.in, or generate your ID through DigiLocker directly</li>
<li>Register using your Aadhaar-linked mobile number (keep this number active — it's required for the registration and verification step)</li>
<li>Your unique 12-digit ABC ID is generated, linked to your APAAR ID</li>
<li>Provide this ID to your university at admission — this is what allows them to deposit your credits into your account as you progress</li>
</ol>
<p>There's no fee at any point in this process. If a university or agent asks you to pay for ABC ID generation or "activation," that's not a legitimate charge — treat it as a red flag about the broader admissions process you're dealing with.</p>

<h2>What ABC ID Is Not</h2>
<p>Worth being precise about this, since the surrounding terminology gets confused constantly:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>It's not the same as UGC-DEB approval.</strong> Having an ABC ID and a university being registered on the ABC platform doesn't tell you whether your specific online course is UGC-DEB approved for your admission session — that's a completely separate check, and the one that actually determines your degree's legal validity. Always <a href="{{ '/ugc-verifier.html' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">verify UGC-DEB approval</a> independently.</li>
<li><strong>It's not a guarantee that any two institutions will accept your credit transfer.</strong> ABC provides the infrastructure and the record; the actual decision to accept transferred credits still depends on the specific sending and receiving institutions' own policies and course equivalence rules.</li>
<li><strong>It's not your marksheet.</strong> You still need your official marksheets and transcripts for admissions and evaluation. ABC ID supplements that record with a structured, portable credit trail — it doesn't replace it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>A Practical Note on Timing</h2>
<p>Set up your ABC ID before you need it, not after. Students most commonly run into friction when they try to arrange a credit transfer or resume a paused degree without having registered earlier — at that point, you're trying to solve an administrative problem under time pressure, which is exactly the wrong moment to be learning how a new government system works. If you're currently enrolled anywhere, generating your ABC ID now costs a few minutes and protects an option you may not need until later.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Do I need an ABC ID for an online degree specifically, or only for regular on-campus programs?</h3>
<p>ABC ID applies across UG, PG, diploma, and certificate programmes regardless of delivery mode — online, distance, or on-campus. If your university is registered on the ABC platform, your online degree's credits are eligible to be recorded the same way.</p>

<h3>Is there a cost to create or maintain an ABC ID?</h3>
<p>No — creating and maintaining an ABC account is free. Any request for payment specifically to "generate" or "activate" your ABC ID is not a legitimate charge.</p>

<h3>Will my online degree be recognized without an ABC ID?</h3>
<p>Your degree's legal validity comes from UGC-DEB approval of your university and course, not from having an ABC ID. However, most UGC-recognized institutions now require an ABC ID as part of standard admission and credit-recording processes, so you'll typically need one regardless.</p>

<h3>Can I transfer credits between any two universities using ABC ID?</h3>
<p>Only if both the sending and receiving institutions are registered on the ABC platform and support the credit-transfer workflow for your specific programs. Confirm this directly with both institutions before assuming a transfer will go through smoothly.</p>

<h2>Bottom Line</h2>
<p>ABC ID isn't the flashiest part of NEP 2020, but it's one of the more genuinely practical ones for the exact kind of student who enrols in an online degree — someone juggling work, family, and study, for whom a pause or a change of plans is a real possibility, not a hypothetical. Set it up early, understand that it works alongside UGC-DEB approval rather than replacing it, and it'll quietly do its job in the background until the day you actually need the flexibility it provides.</p>
