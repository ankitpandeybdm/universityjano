<p>Every year, a few students reach the final step of an online degree admission and get stuck on one field: <strong>DEB ID</strong>. With the admission window for the August 2026 session closing soon, this is the one piece of paperwork that can quietly derail an otherwise perfect enrolment. The good news: it takes about 15 minutes if your documents are in order. Here's exactly what it is and how to get it done.</p>

<h2>What Is a DEB ID (and Why Did UGC Make It Compulsory)?</h2>
<p>DEB ID is a unique identification number issued through the <strong>UGC Distance Education Bureau (DEB)</strong> portal. Any learner who wants to take admission in an Open and Distance Learning (ODL) or Online programme is required to create one first.</p>
<p>The purpose is simple and student-friendly: it ensures you are enrolling in a programme offered by an institution that is actually recognised by UGC. In other words, it is a built-in safety check against fake or unapproved "online degrees" — the biggest risk in this space.</p>
<p>Your DEB ID is generated using your <strong>ABC ID</strong>, and it is a one-time registration rather than something you renew every year.</p>

<h2>The Deadline: 13 October 2026</h2>
<p>The DEB portal currently displays that the last date to admit learners in ODL/Online programmes for the <strong>August 2026 session is 13 October 2026</strong>. Some earlier articles quoted 15 September as the cut-off, so if you see conflicting dates online, treat the official DEB portal notice as the final word and confirm directly with your chosen university.</p>
<p>Practically, this means you have roughly two weeks. Universities also need time to verify your documents, so waiting until the last week is a risky plan.</p>

<h2>ABC ID vs APAAR ID vs DEB ID: What's the Difference?</h2>
<p>These three names get mixed up constantly, so here is the short version:</p>

<tr style="background:#0b1b3d;color:#fff;"><td style="padding:0.6rem 0.9rem;font-weight:700;">ID</td><td style="padding:0.6rem 0.9rem;font-weight:700;">What it is</td><td style="padding:0.6rem 0.9rem;font-weight:700;">Where you create it</td></tr>

<p>The order matters: <strong>APAAR/ABC ID first, DEB ID second</strong>, and only then your admission form.</p>

<h2>How to Create Your DEB ID, Step by Step</h2>

<h3>Step 1: Create or locate your ABC ID</h3>
<p>Log in to DigiLocker and generate your ABC ID if you don't already have one. If you studied in a school or college recently, you may already have an APAAR/ABC ID — check DigiLocker before creating a new one.</p>

<h3>Step 2: Check your APAAR/ABC card for errors</h3>
<p>This is the step most students skip, and it causes the most problems. Open your card and verify your <strong>name, date of birth and gender</strong>. If anything is wrong, get it corrected first through the ABC support ticket system or the DigiLocker app. The ABC helpline is <strong>1800 889 3511</strong>. Only after the card is correct should you move on.</p>

<h3>Step 3: Register on the DEB portal</h3>
<p>Go to <strong>deb.ugc.ac.in</strong> and open the "Get Your DEB ID" section. You will be asked for your ABC ID, your name as per Aadhaar, year of birth and gender, along with consent details and a captcha. Enter these exactly as they appear on your ID.</p>

<h3>Step 4: Note your DEB ID and use it in the admission form</h3>
<p>Once generated, your DEB ID appears on screen along with your name, date of birth and the university and course details once you are admitted. Save it — your university will ask for it during enrolment.</p>
<p>If you already created a DEB ID but spotted a mistake later, there is an "Update Your DEB Details" option on the same portal. The same rule applies: fix your APAAR card first, then update the DEB ID.</p>

<h2>Mistakes That Delay Admissions</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Name mismatch:</strong> the name on your APAAR/ABC card, Aadhaar and academic documents should line up. Small spelling differences can hold up verification.</li>
<li><strong>Starting too late:</strong> correcting an ABC/APAAR record can take time. Start today, not on deadline day.</li>
<li><strong>Paying before verifying:</strong> creating a DEB ID does not replace checking the university itself. Always confirm that the specific programme is recognised for your session.</li>
<li><strong>Trusting agents blindly:</strong> your DEB ID is created by you, using your own details. Be cautious of anyone who wants to handle it "on your behalf" without your involvement.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why "Approved University" Isn't Enough</h2>
<p>UGC recognition for online programmes works <strong>per university, per programme, per academic session</strong>. UGC also ran its application window for institutions seeking ODL and online recognition for the 2026-27 cycle in September, which is a reminder that approval is renewed and reviewed regularly rather than granted once forever.</p>
<p>That is why the DEB portal lists recognised programmes separately. Before paying any fee, look for your exact course on that list. And treat these as red flags:</p>
<ul>
<li>A degree promised in "one sitting" or a suspiciously short "fast-track" timeline</li>
<li>Unusually low fees for a complete degree</li>
<li>Programmes that need mandatory clinical or lab practice (such as medical degrees) being sold as fully online</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Is DEB ID mandatory for every online degree?</h3>
<p>Yes. Any learner taking admission in a UGC-recognised ODL or online programme is required to create a DEB ID. Foreign nationals are the exception.</p>

<h3>Can I get a DEB ID without an ABC ID?</h3>
<p>No. The DEB ID is generated using your ABC ID, so create that first through DigiLocker.</p>

<h3>What if my name or date of birth is wrong on my APAAR card?</h3>
<p>Correct it first via the ABC support ticket system or DigiLocker, and only then create or update your DEB ID. Doing it in the reverse order tends to create mismatches that are harder to fix.</p>

<h3>Does a DEB ID guarantee my degree is valid?</h3>
<p>It confirms you are enrolling in a programme recognised on the DEB system, which is a strong safeguard, but you should still verify your exact programme and session yourself. Our <a href="{{ '/ugc-verifier.html' | relative_url }}" style="color:#1d4ed8;font-weight:600;">UGC verifier</a> is a good starting point.</p>

<h3>Is the last date really 13 October 2026?</h3>
<p>That is the date shown on the official DEB portal at the time of writing. Dates can be revised, and some sources still quote earlier ones, so confirm with the DEB portal and your university before making a final decision.</p>

<h2>Bottom Line</h2>
<p>The DEB ID is not a hurdle to fear; it is one of the few genuinely useful protections students have against unrecognised online degrees. Create your ABC/APAAR ID, fix any errors, generate your DEB ID, and verify your programme, in that order. With the August 2026 session deadline approaching, the best time to start is today.</p>
