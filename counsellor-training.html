A counsellor decides whether an enquiry becomes an admission.

Students and parents have doubts about fees, approvals and value. The counsellor on the call is the one who answers them. When that person explains clearly, listens first and follows up on time, more enquiries turn into enrolments.

UniversityJano guides students through regular, online and certification courses every day. This training brings that counselling experience to your team.

Problems we fix in counselling teams

If you recognise even a few of these in your team, the training is built for you.

  • Slow or generic first reply

    Enquiries wait for hours, then get a copied brochure message instead of a real conversation.

  • Pitching before listening

    The counsellor starts explaining the programme without knowing what the student actually needs.

  • Fear of the fee conversation

    Counsellors avoid talking about fees, or state them in a way that raises doubts.

  • Weak answers to objections

    Doubts about fees, validity and placements get defensive replies and the student goes quiet.

  • Overpromising

    Promises such as guaranteed jobs win a call but cause cancellations, complaints and a damaged reputation.

  • No follow-up routine

    Interested students are forgotten after one call because there is no system for the next step.

The curriculum, in the order a conversation happens

Each stage of the counselling journey has its own skill. We work through them one by one with your team, with practice at every stage.

  1. Replying to the first enquiry

    The first message sets the tone for the whole conversation.

    • Reply quickly and personally, not with a copy-paste brochure
    • Open a conversation with a question the student can answer
    • Move the student from form or chat to a proper call

  2. Understanding the student

    The right recommendation starts with the right questions.

    • Discovery questions about goals, work status, budget and timeline
    • Finding out who really decides, including parents and employers
    • Matching the student to the right programme instead of the easiest one

  3. Explaining the programme

    Accurate information builds trust faster than any pitch.

    • A clear order for explaining eligibility, approvals, exam mode and fees
    • Where students can verify approvals, such as the official UGC-DEB portal
    • What to say when the counsellor does not know the answer

  4. Handling objections

    An objection usually means the student is still interested but unsure.

    • Acknowledge the doubt, ask what is behind it, then answer
    • Honest replies on fees, validity, placements and competitors
    • Staying calm and polite when the student pushes back

  5. Talking about fees and EMI

    Money conversations go well when they are plain and complete.

    • Stating the total fee clearly before talking about instalments
    • Presenting EMI as a payment option, not as a lower price
    • Mentioning any extra costs upfront, so there are no surprises

  6. Following up

    Most admissions are closed in the follow-up, not the first call.

    • A simple follow-up schedule your whole team can use
    • Messages that add value instead of repeating "any update?"
    • Handling "I will think about it" and reviving cold leads

  7. Closing the admission

    The sale is not done until the student is enrolled and settled.

    • Guiding the student through documents, application and payment
    • Keeping the student comfortable between payment and first class
    • Asking happy students for referrals
  8. Throughout: honest counselling. No false promises such as guaranteed placements, because trust protects your institution's name.

Objection handling, up close

Five doubts counsellors hear every week, and how a trained counsellor approaches each one.

"Fees zyada hai."

Untrained

Defends the price or repeats the number.

Trained

Acknowledges the concern, asks about budget, explains the total fee and then the payment options.

"Kya online degree valid hai?"

Untrained

Says "Haan haan, bilkul valid hai" and moves on.

Trained

Explains the approvals clearly and shows the student how to verify them on the official portal.

"Placement guarantee hai?"

Untrained

Promises a job to win the admission.

Trained

Explains what placement support is, what it is not, and never promises a job.

"Soch ke batata hoon."

Untrained

Ends the call and never follows up.

Trained

Asks what is still unclear, answers it, and fixes a time for the next conversation.

"Dusri university sasti hai."

Untrained

Runs down the competitor.

Trained

Compares fairly on approval, curriculum, support and total cost, and helps the student decide.

These are illustrative approaches. In the training, your team practises them in their own words, on your programmes.
