How We Ranked: Our Methodology

We rank universities in two stages: a pass/fail gate, then a 100-point score.

Stage 1: The gate (pass or fail)

UGC-DEB approval. A university only qualifies if the online programme is entitled or recognised by UGC-DEB for the current session. This is not scored, because a degree without it is not valid, however good the rest looks. As of the latest UGC lists we reviewed, 113 institutions are entitled to offer online programmes, so passing this gate still leaves a lot of choice. Approval applies per programme and per session, so always confirm your exact course with our UGC-DEB verifier.

What this method can and cannot tell you

  • Fee data is messy. The same programme is often quoted at very different prices across websites, because of specialisation tiers, early-bird discounts, and whether exam fees are included. We therefore show ranges and score on the midpoint. In one case we found a comparison site quoting a fee far below the university's own official page, which is why we cross-checked each fee against at least two sources.
  • Placement numbers are self-reported. Hiring-partner counts come from the universities. They tell you a placement cell exists, not how many students get jobs. Ask for last year's placement report before you enrol.
  • "Not confirmed" is not a negative. Where we could not verify a detail from an official source, we scored it conservatively and marked it in the table. A university with more publicly documented details can therefore score higher than one that simply publishes less. Treat those cells as questions to ask the admissions team.
  • Fee weighting favours affordability. Premium brands such as NMIMS and Symbiosis score lower on cost by design. If brand matters more to you than price, adjust the weights.

Pros and Cons of Each University

A ranking is only useful if it tells you the downsides too. Here is what each university does well, and where it falls short.

1. GLA University Online (Score: 84)

Pros:

  • Among the lowest fees of any private NAAC A+ university on this list
  • Exams are 100% online and web-proctored, with no travel to an exam centre
  • Live classes and doubt-clearing sessions through the GLA Portal, not only recorded lectures
  • No-cost EMI available, and no entrance exam

Cons:

  • Smaller national brand than Amity, Manipal or NMIMS
  • Reported hiring network (350+ partners) is smaller than the largest players
  • Fee figures vary across sources, and placement numbers are self-reported

Full details in our GLA University Online guide.

2. Chandigarh University Online (Score: 79)

Pros:

  • Very wide choice of specialisations, including dual specialisation options
  • Zero-cost EMI and an early-bird discount reported on its official pages
  • Industry certifications are reported as bundled with some programmes
  • Mid-range fees for an NIRF-ranked university

Cons:

  • NAAC A+ rather than A++
  • Published fees differ a lot by source and by whether the early-bird discount is applied
  • Hiring-partner counts are quoted inconsistently across sources (300+ to 900+), so treat them cautiously

3. Amity University Online (Score: 78)

Pros:

  • Large reported recruiter network (450+ partners)
  • Zero-cost EMI, plus a discount for paying the full fee upfront
  • International recognition (WES, and QAA/WASC as stated by the university), useful if you may study or work abroad
  • Extensive recorded lecture library alongside live sessions

Cons:

  • Higher fee at the top of its range (up to about ₹2.25L)
  • NAAC A+ rather than A++
  • Published fees vary widely between sources

4. Jain University Online (Score: 70)

Pros:

  • NAAC A++
  • Exams can be taken online with a laptop and webcam
  • Around 10 specialisations, including an entrepreneurship track

Cons:

  • We could not confirm EMI terms from an official source
  • No verified hiring-partner figure in the sources we reviewed
  • Bengaluru startup ecosystem benefits are strongest for learners connected to that region

5. Amrita Online (Score: 70)

Pros:

  • NAAC A++ with a high NIRF overall rank as stated by the university
  • Zero-cost EMI shown on official programme pages

  • Industry-linked specialisations with Grant Thornton

Cons:

  • Fees are at the upper end of this list (₹1.70–2.60L on the university's own pages)

One thing we learned while compiling this: a popular comparison site listed Amrita's MBA at ₹0.8–1.2L, but the university's own pages show ₹1.70L and above. Always check the official fee page.

6. LPU Online (Score: 69)

Cons:

Frequently Asked Questions

Which university ranks first on this list, and is it best for everyone?

On our scoring formula, GLA University Online ranks first with 84 out of 100, mainly because it combines a NAAC A+ grade with low fees, confirmed no-cost EMI and remote proctored exams. It is not the best choice for everyone. Learners who prioritise a premium management brand may prefer NMIMS or Amity, and those who need the lowest possible cost may prefer IGNOU.

How do I check whether an online degree is UGC-DEB approved?

Open the UGC-DEB portal at deb.ugc.ac.in and look for your exact programme in the recognised or entitled programmes list for your admission session. Approval is granted per programme and per session, so a university being approved in general is not enough. You also need to create a DEB ID linked to your ABC ID before admission.

Is a NAAC A++ university always better than an A+ university?

No. The NAAC grade measures an institution's overall quality, not the quality of a specific online programme, and it does not directly determine placements. It is one useful signal among several, together with UGC-DEB approval, fees, placement support and how you prefer to learn. NAAC is also changing its grading system, so grades may be presented differently in future.

Are 0% EMI online degrees really free of interest?

Zero-cost EMI usually means you do not pay separate interest on the instalments, but the cost may be built into the fee or offset by a discount you lose. For example, one university lists a discount for paying the full fee upfront, which means the EMI route can cost more overall. Always ask whether processing fees, taxes or other charges apply, and get the total payable amount in writing.

Are online degrees valid for government jobs?

A degree from a UGC-DEB entitled or recognised programme is treated as equivalent to a regular degree, so it is generally accepted for jobs and higher studies. However, some recruitment notifications specify their own conditions, so always read the exact eligibility wording in the notification before you enrol.

Do online degree exams take place at home?

It depends on the university. Several universities, including GLA, Jain and Manipal Jaipur, report remote online-proctored exams that you take with a laptop and webcam. Others are reported to hold exams at partner centres. Confirm the exam mode for your programme before enrolling, especially if you travel or live far from a city.

Why do fees for the same course differ so much between websites?

Published fees can differ because of specialisation tiers, early-bird or scholarship discounts, whether exam and registration fees are included, and how often comparison sites update their data. That is why this page shows ranges and scores on the midpoint. Always confirm the current fee directly with the university before paying.

How often is this list updated?

We revise this page when UGC-DEB publishes updated entitlement lists or when a university changes its fees or schemes. The last updated date at the top of the page shows when the data was most recently reviewed.
